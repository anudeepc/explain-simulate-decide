# Shot types

You choose the shot type, and the renderer decides the camera, framing and timing. Use one idea per shot.

| Shot | What it is for | Camera | What moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| `establish` | Opening: show the whole set and ask the question | Wide, slow push-in | Nothing, the set is introduced |
| `introduce` | Meet an actor and what it stands for | Dolly to a ¾ angle, subject on a third | The actor's value sweeps through its range |
| `focus` | Stay with one actor to make a point about it | Close, slight angle | The actor, or the lever it depends on, sweeps |
| `cause` | Show that X changes Y (`focus` → `affects`) | Two-shot of both actors | The cause sweeps, a light flow travels to the effect, and the effect reacts |
| `compare` | Show two states of one thing ("a little vs a lot") | Front-on, medium | The `drive` input sweeps low → high |
| `turn` | Correct the common misconception. **Exactly one.** | Holds at an angle, then a slow reveal; the longest shot | `drive` sweeps to show the surprise |
| `handoff` | Last shot: hand the model to the viewer | Settles into the Simulate view | Sliders appear next |

## A reliable shape (6–10 shots)

1. `establish`: the question.
2. `introduce` or `cause` for each main lever and condition, following the order the cause and effect happens.
3. `turn`: the insight.
4. `compare` or `focus` on the outcome(s).
5. `handoff`.

## Timing (automatic)

- **Length:** a shot lasts as long as its caption takes to read (about 2.6 words per second) plus a 1.6 s hold, and at least 3.5 s.
- **`turn`** gets an extra 1.5 s.
- **Narration clips,** when present, can lengthen shots.
- **After Explain:** the recording adds a 12 s Simulate segment and a 10 s Decide segment.

Six to ten shots give a film of about 50–75 seconds.
