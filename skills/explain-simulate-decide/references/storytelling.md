# Story: direct the film like a filmmaker

A model explains. A story makes the viewer care enough to follow the model. Before you map anything, decide the story that the film tells. Think like a great director of family films. Such a director shows a person we recognise, an ordinary moment, a question that grows and a discovery. Then the film goes back to the person, who now sees their situation in a new way.

## The story in five lines

Write these five lines first. They go in `story`.

1. **The hero.** One person that the viewer can stand in for. The hero is a character on the set: "you", a household, a patient, a commuter. Specific and ordinary is better than general. For example, "a kit lands on your doormat" is better than "screening programmes". In the screenplay, the hero is a node (`story.hero`).
2. **What the hero wants.** For example: a lower bill, a good night's sleep, to find an illness early or to be free of debt.
3. **What the hero does not know yet.** The misconception, or the hidden part of the system. This becomes the `turn`.
4. **The discovery.** The moment when the world shows the hero what really happens. For example: two streams side by side, a crowd that splits, a jar that never fills.
5. **How it ends.** Back with the hero, who now understands the choice. The film gives the choice to the viewer. The film never makes the choice for the viewer.

Then write the **logline**: 35 words or fewer, in short sentences, with all five lines. Example: "A kit arrives on your doormat. Most kits find nothing. Most people who go for more tests do not have cancer. So what does the test really change for you?"

## How a director shoots the film

- **Open small.** Start on the hero in an ordinary moment. Do not start on a statement about the topic. The first shot after `establish` is about the hero. It can `introduce` the hero, or `follow` something that arrives at the hero's door.
- **Follow the hero through the world.** The camera goes where the hero's things go. Examples:
  - the kit to the lab
  - the power from the roof to the home
  - the money from the card to the bank.

  Put the shots in the order that things happen to the hero.
- **Go wide, then come back.** Move the camera out to the big picture (100 people, 25 years, the whole grid). Then end close on the hero again. The last shot before `handoff` is about the hero. The validator checks both ends.
- **Hold the turn.** One shot before the turn, show what the hero expects. Then let the turn land on a held, quiet shot. The `turn` gets more time on screen. Do not put a second idea in it.
- **Show, then say.** The picture explains. The caption tells the viewer what to see and why it matters. Never write a caption about what the viewer can plainly see ("Here is a house").
- **Honest stakes.** Show real consequences. Tell them calmly. Use no fear, no urgency and no villains. Respect the viewer's intelligence and their right to decide.

## Show every step: don't skip

Sometimes the film follows something that a person does: a test, a form, a claim or an installation. Then show **every real step, in the order that it happens**. Use the steps and the order of the authoritative source. After the film, the viewer must be able to do each step. If you skip a step, the viewer must guess what happens in the gap. For example, the film shows "you take a sample", and then the post box.

- **One step, one beat.** Take the steps from your research ("What really happens" in SKILL.md). Quote them in `source.quotes`. Make each step a beat with its own `caption`. Examples: "First, write the date on the sample bottle." "Open the bottle. The lid has a sample stick."
- **Look closely at small things.** For a step that the hands do (write, open, seal), give the beat `"camera": "detail"`. The camera moves in to the hands and back out. It never cuts.
- **Give each step time.** A beat with a caption stays on screen until the viewer can read it. Do not put two steps in one line. If a scene needs more than 8 beats, split it into two scenes in the same place.
- **No jumps in time or place without words.** If days go by or the hero goes to a new place, the caption says so. Example: "The result comes by letter, usually within two weeks."
- **Keep the steps that matter for the decision.** Keep them accurate. Do not invent steps that the source does not describe. Sometimes you cannot show a step: it is private, or the engine has no prop for it. Then say the step in the caption. At the same time, the hero does the nearest action.

## Captions

Captions are the narration. Viewers can turn the captions off, so the picture must also tell the story. When the captions are on, they must read like the narrator of a film.

- **Present tense, close to the hero:** "The kit arrives. You post a tiny sample back." "Your panels wake up with the sun."
- **One image, one idea, 12 words or fewer.** A caption names what happens on screen now.
- **Rhythm.** Change the length. A short line after two longer lines lands hard: "Most find nothing."
- **Plain words.** Explain a term the first time that you use it ("a colonoscopy, a camera check of the bowel").
- **The last line gives the choice to the viewer:** "Now make it yours." "Your numbers decide the rest."
- **ASD-STE100.** Captions follow the rule "All viewer text is ASD-STE100" in `writing-rules.md`.

## Cast: the right characters

- **Recognisable with the sound off.** For each character, ask what it really looks like. If you do not know, look it up. For example, a home test kit is a small tube in an envelope. A heat pump is a box with a fan. Choose the prop or `kind` that is nearest to the real thing. Or make a simple `recipe`.
- **The hero is always someone that the viewer can stand in for.** The hero is a `person`, a `body` or the viewer's `home`. Or the hero is a `crowd` of people like the viewer.
- **Few characters, each with a job** (see "The best part is no part" in SKILL.md). If two characters have the same role, merge them.

## Colour: the tone

Each film has a clean white set. The props give the colour. One accent colour (`tone`) suits the topic:

| `tone` | For |
|---|---|
| `health` | medicine, screening, sleep, the body |
| `money` | debt, savings, bills, pay |
| `energy` | power, solar, fuel, batteries |
| `water` | rain, tanks, rivers, water use |
| `climate` | emissions, transport, nature |
| `civic` | laws, taxes, public services |
| `food` | diet, cooking, groceries |
| `home` | heating, housing, the household |
| `neutral` | anything else |
